Best (First) Solo Trip Destinations for Women Over 30

12.08.202614 min read

Maybe you’ve been thinking about travelling alone for months.

You save beautiful hotels on Instagram, send yourself restaurant recommendations and occasionally find yourself looking at flights — only to close the tab when it starts feeling a little too real.

Because wanting to travel alone and actually knowing where you would feel comfortable travelling alone are two very different things. Especially for your first trip.

The good news is that your first solo trip doesn’t have to be particularly adventurous to be meaningful. And you certainly don’t need to backpack around Southeast Asia for three months to qualify as a solo traveller.

For many women in their 30s, 40s and beyond, solo travel looks very different. It might mean checking into a beautiful hotel in Lisbon for four nights. Spending mornings walking along Sydney’s coastline. Taking yourself for dinner somewhere overlooking Dubai’s skyline. Drinking tea in the Sri Lankan mountains. Or wandering around Singapore knowing you can change your plans whenever you feel like it.

The important part isn’t how adventurous the trip looks from the outside. It’s how the trip makes you feel.

And if you’re planning your first solo trip, choosing the right destination can make a huge difference. Here are five destinations I particularly like for women considering travelling alone for the first time — and, more importantly, who I think each one suits best.

1. Portugal

Best for: the woman who wants independence without feeling too far outside her comfort zone.

If you’re intrigued by solo travel but still want your first experience to feel fairly easy, Portugal is a very good place to start. There is enough novelty for it to feel like an adventure, without requiring you to completely throw yourself into the unknown.

Lisbon gives you colourful neighbourhoods, beautiful cafés, wine bars, viewpoints and plenty to explore on foot. Porto offers a slightly smaller, atmospheric city experience. And if what you really need is space, sunshine and ocean rather than sightseeing, you can move south towards the Algarve or build a slower coastal element into the trip. Portugal’s official tourism organisation covers everything from Lisbon and Porto to the Algarve, Alentejo, Madeira and the Azores, which means the country can support very different styles of trip within one destination.

Portugal works particularly well if part of your nervousness comes from wondering: “What am I actually going to do with myself all day?”

There is an easy rhythm available here. Wake up slowly. Walk somewhere for coffee and a pastel de nata. Explore a neighbourhood. Stop somewhere beautiful for lunch. Browse a shop you would normally walk straight past because somebody else was in a hurry.

You don’t have to manufacture a profound solo-travel experience. You can simply live differently for a few days.

Portugal might be right for you if:

  • You want culture, food and beautiful surroundings without an overly intense itinerary
  • You enjoy walking and discovering places slowly
  • You would like a combination of city and coast
  • You are nervous about travelling alone and want your first trip to feel manageable
  • You want independence, but you’re not necessarily looking for a huge adventure

A Grounding to Go-style Portugal trip might look like: a few days in Lisbon with mornings left intentionally slow, one or two pre-planned activities to give your days structure, beautiful restaurants where eating alone doesn’t feel intimidating, a day by the ocean, and enough unplanned time to follow whatever you feel like doing once you’re there.

Portugal is the gentle introduction to solo travel.

2. Sri Lanka

Best for: the woman who feels stuck and needs to experience something completely different.

Sri Lanka is a very different first solo trip. I wouldn’t necessarily recommend it to someone whose main priority is having the easiest possible experience. I would recommend it to someone who wants to come home feeling like she has actually been somewhere.

There is an extraordinary amount of variety packed into one island: beaches, tea-covered mountains, wildlife, ancient sites, Ayurveda, nature and cultural experiences. Sri Lanka Tourism highlights exactly this combination of beach, wildlife, adventure, heritage, Ayurveda and scenic landscapes.

You can wake up surrounded by misty hills in the centre of the country and, a few days later, be barefoot beside the Indian Ocean. And psychologically, that amount of novelty can be powerful when everyday life has started feeling repetitive.

Sometimes what you need isn’t rest. You need new input. Different smells. Different landscapes. Different food. Different routines. A reminder that the world is much bigger than whatever has been consuming your thoughts at home.

For a first-time solo female traveller, though, Sri Lanka benefits from more thoughtful planning than some of the other destinations on this list. Sri Lanka Tourism itself says women can travel around the country alone relatively easily, while also noting that women have experienced harassment in some beach and tourist areas and recommending sensible precautions.

That doesn’t automatically make it the wrong destination. It means that where you stay, how you travel between locations and how your itinerary is structured matter. And for some women, having those decisions made carefully beforehand is what transforms the trip from intimidating to exciting.

Sri Lanka might be right for you if:

  • You are going through a period where life feels stagnant
  • You want adventure, but still appreciate comfort
  • Nature makes you feel grounded
  • You want a trip that combines several completely different environments
  • You like the idea of culture, wellness, wildlife and beaches rather than spending an entire holiday in one city
  • You’re comfortable with a little more unpredictability

A Grounding to Go-style Sri Lanka trip might look like: a thoughtfully planned route rather than trying to see the entire island. Perhaps tea country and slow mornings in the hills, a beautiful train journey, a few days near the coast, an Ayurveda treatment, wildlife or hiking, and carefully chosen transport and accommodation so that the adventurous parts feel exciting rather than stressful.

Sri Lanka is the trip for when you need to feel awake again.

3. Dubai

Best for: the woman who wants to try solo travel without sacrificing comfort.

Dubai is an interesting choice for a first solo trip because you can make it incredibly easy. You can have the unfamiliarity of travelling somewhere alone without simultaneously dealing with complicated logistics, difficult transportation or a lack of things to do.

There are beaches, desert experiences, spas, restaurants, cafés, shopping, wellness, nightlife and hotels at almost every level of luxury. Dubai’s official tourism site also specifically publishes guidance for solo female travellers and describes the city as welcoming and highly safe for women travelling alone.

For someone who is nervous about solo travel, that convenience matters. Your first trip doesn’t have to test your resilience. In fact, if you’re already going through a stressful period in your life, deliberately choosing somewhere that removes unnecessary friction may be exactly what you need.

Dubai also works particularly well for women who think they might feel awkward spending time alone. Because you can keep yourself as occupied — or as unoccupied — as you want.

You could spend the morning by the pool, go for lunch, book a spa treatment, join a desert experience before sunset and have dinner overlooking the city. Or you could do almost nothing. Both count.

There are local customs worth understanding, particularly around clothing and behaviour in certain settings, which is why it’s still worth preparing before you arrive. Dubai’s official visitor information includes dedicated advice on local etiquette, practical information and what to wear.

Dubai might be right for you if:

  • Safety and predictability are major concerns
  • You enjoy comfort and beautiful hotels
  • You don’t want to rough it just because you’re travelling alone
  • You like having plenty of activities available
  • You want sunshine, wellness, restaurants and experiences in one destination
  • You are nervous about your first solo trip and want something logistically straightforward

A Grounding to Go-style Dubai trip might look like: a hotel in the neighbourhood that best matches your personality rather than simply the most famous area, carefully chosen restaurants where you’ll feel comfortable dining alone, a spa afternoon, beach time, one experience that pushes you slightly outside your normal routine, and a desert evening when you want to feel a little further away from everyday life.

Dubai is the comfortable first step into independence.

4. Sydney

Best for: the woman who needs space, movement and a reminder that life can feel good.

Some destinations invite you to sit still. Sydney makes you want to move. Walk along the coastline. Swim. Get coffee. Take a ferry. Sit beside the harbour. Go for breakfast. Walk some more.

Sydney’s official tourism resources emphasise the city’s combination of coastline, green spaces and outdoor activities including hiking, surfing, cycling and snorkelling. Tourism Australia also actively recommends Australia for solo holidays and notes the availability of activities, tours and classes for travellers who want some social interaction along the way.

That makes Sydney particularly interesting if you’ve spent the last few months feeling mentally stuck or physically disconnected from yourself.

Not every life transition requires sitting in a silent retreat thinking about your feelings. Sometimes you need sunlight. You need to walk until your head feels quieter. You need to swim in the ocean at 8am, eat something delicious afterwards and remember what it feels like to have an entire day that belongs to you.

Sydney can also work well for women who are worried they might become lonely travelling alone. There is enough happening around you that being alone doesn’t necessarily feel isolating.

You can join a walking tour, take a surf lesson, spend the day at the beach, visit galleries, walk through different neighbourhoods or simply sit somewhere with a coffee and watch everyday life happening around you. Sydney’s official tourism site even lists small-group walking experiences specifically suited to solo travellers.

Sydney might be right for you if:

  • Movement improves your mood
  • You love being close to water
  • You want a city, but don’t want to spend your entire trip surrounded by buildings
  • You like cafés, beaches, neighbourhoods and outdoor activities
  • You want opportunities to meet people without needing to be constantly social
  • You’re craving energy rather than complete stillness

A Grounding to Go-style Sydney trip might look like: morning coastal walks, neighbourhood cafés, a beach afternoon with absolutely nothing scheduled afterwards, a ferry ride across the harbour, one social activity if you want connection, great food and perhaps a day outside the city when you need more nature.

Sydney is the trip for getting yourself moving again.

5. Singapore

Best for: the woman who is curious about solo travel but wants things to feel organised.

If the thought of landing alone somewhere unfamiliar makes you immediately start imagining everything that could go wrong, Singapore could be an excellent first destination. It gives you a significant cultural change without necessarily giving you the logistical chaos that some people associate with long-haul travel.

The Singapore Tourism Board has an entire official itinerary specifically designed around travelling to Singapore alone, including accommodation, activities, tours and advice for solo visitors.

And there is something particularly reassuring about a destination where you can build your days around clearly defined neighbourhoods and experiences. One morning might mean wandering around Tiong Bahru. Another could mean gardens, museums and architecture. Then there are hawker centres, rooftop bars, beautiful hotels, heritage neighbourhoods and an enormous variety of food.

It is also an interesting first solo destination psychologically because it allows you to experience independence without excessive unpredictability. For an anxious traveller, that can be the sweet spot.

You still get the confidence boost of navigating a foreign city alone. You still decide where to go, what to eat and how you spend your time. But you don’t necessarily need every day to feel like a logistical challenge.

Singapore might be right for you if:

  • You like organised environments
  • Uncertainty makes you more anxious than being alone itself
  • Food is a major part of how you enjoy travelling
  • You want culture and novelty without a particularly chaotic trip
  • You enjoy cities, architecture, neighbourhoods and beautiful hotels
  • You want to test how solo travel feels before attempting something more adventurous

A Grounding to Go-style Singapore trip might look like: a hotel chosen around the neighbourhood atmosphere you would actually enjoy, one area of the city explored properly each day rather than rushing between attractions, food experiences, gardens, beautiful evening spots and a balance between independent exploration and activities that give your day some structure.

Singapore is the confidence-building first solo trip.

So… which one should you choose?

Here is where most “best solo travel destination” lists fall short. Because all five destinations can be wonderful. That doesn’t mean all five are wonderful for you right now.

If you’re burned out and already overstimulated, sending yourself into an incredibly busy itinerary because TikTok told you it was the perfect solo trip probably isn’t particularly helpful. If you’ve spent the last year feeling bored and stuck, booking seven days at an isolated wellness resort might not give you what you’re craving either. And if your biggest fear is being alone, choosing somewhere where you need a car to reach almost everything could unintentionally make your first trip harder than it needs to be.

Instead, ask yourself: what do I want to feel more of when I come home? Calm? Confidence? Energy? Curiosity? Independence? Connection? Adventure? Or simply relief?

Then ask: what has been missing from my life lately? That answer can tell you far more about where you should travel than another list of the “10 best destinations for solo women.”

If I were simplifying the five destinations in this guide, I’d think about them like this:

  • Portugal: I want to ease myself into solo travel.
  • Sri Lanka: I feel stuck and need something completely different.
  • Dubai: I want independence, but I still want comfort.
  • Sydney: I need energy, movement and the outdoors.
  • Singapore: I want novelty without too much uncertainty.

And of course, even that is only the beginning. Because two women choosing Portugal could still need completely different trips. One might need cafés, coastal walks and long afternoons with nowhere to be. The other might need cooking classes, wine tastings, day trips and enough structure that she doesn’t have time to overthink being alone.

The destination matters. But the way you experience it matters just as much.
